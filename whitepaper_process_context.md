## Process-model research

### MARE-Madeira "Impossible Things Workshop" — the exemplar Mabel points at
Source (white paper / process review): <https://twilighted.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/White-Paper_The-Impossible-Things-Workshop-2025.docx.pdf>. Output advocacy paper (Cell/Trends, low-cost deep-sea research, broad audience, higher-level language): <https://www.cell.com/trends-open/fulltext/S3117-3470(26)00017-9>.

- **Format:** 2.5-day retreat, 40 people, 12 countries, deliberately diverse/cross-sector; almost entirely small-group work, no lectures; chairs clustered for small groups.
- **Structure:** 6 pre-defined *challenge areas*, each with 3–4 key questions, each led by a moderator + ~5 participants. Small-team brainstorming; teams present to the whole group for feedback; individual brainstorming; "brainstorming hikes"; a crazy-mode pitch competition; a final new-team one-year-planning round led by idea enthusiasts.
- **Input vs. output structure (useful design point):** the 6 challenge areas were scaffolding to organize the room; the write-up (Part VI) *regrouped* the results as a Problem → Solution table around cross-cutting problems (cost, power, comms, data) that recurred across all areas. Expect the same for us: seed taxonomy to run the room, emergent regrouping in the paper — so capture notes/transcripts to make regrouping possible.
- **Their lessons learned:** whole-group intro to all challenge areas before splitting; state brainstorming ground rules so all voices are heard; balance in-expertise vs. out-of-expertise placement; standardize note-taking (audio + auto-transcripts); build explicit future-collaboration mechanisms; 4–5 groups of 5–6 beat 6 groups of 6.
- **Output/drafting process:** one strong lead author drives; a 1–5 person core drafts a Google-Doc outline; ~20 reviewers add ideas / answer inline questions; 5–8 named section authors write async over weeks; lead author heavily revises to a tight word limit and one coherent voice; iterate to a journal; ~1 year (arXiv would be faster). Democratic and transparent — the lead kept everyone informed at every step.

### US National Robotics Roadmap — the contrast
Call for ~60 two-page white papers → three 1–2 day in-person workshops → synthesis by a small core of editors → publication; months to ~6 months; coordinated by Henrik Christensen (UCSD). The synthesis stage is comparatively opaque, which is the part to avoid. Refs: <https://cra.org/ccc/roadmaps/robotics-roadmap/>, <https://cra.org/crn/2023/08/call-for-white-papers-for-the-2024-robotics-roadmap/>.
